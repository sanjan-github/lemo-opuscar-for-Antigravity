No.11927/28

Wings

3DB&WSilent-era 3D Dogfight, Tinted Ortho B&W

  • three.js 3D
  • Orthochromatic black and white
  • Silent-film tinting
  • Projector wear and iris

A three.js dogfight graded like a 1927 orthochromatic print: white skies, deep blacks, an amber tint, gate weave and scratches. Wings is remembered for aerial combat shot from cameras strapped to the planes, so most cameras here ride on the planes.

Style prompt

Smooth-shaded 3D graded as a silent-era orthochromatic print: high contrast, reds go black. Sky near-white, ground in readable mid-greys, deep blacks (25-35% of frame) only in silhouettes. Tint: blend whites half-way toward amber #FFCC80, so the sky is pale cream-amber, never solid orange; blacks stay black. Only the hero the story centres on keeps colour, whole figure at ~70% saturation (e.g. #D97757); every lamp, fire or other light source stays grey. 24 fps gate weave (±1 px), flicker (±5%), fine grain (±4%), 1-3 thin scratches, sparse dust, rounded corners, vignette; open on a 1 s iris. Signature shot: a camera rigged to something moving with the subject, rig in frame; then chase and POV. Cut every 2-3 s; white out through cloud, smoke or glare. Reference: William Wellman, *Wings* (1927). Avoid: motion blur, colour in the greys.

Shots & direction

  1. open0.9s

    Head-on over the cowling: Clawd in a leather cap with goggles pushed up sits between the struts; the iris opens and the camera pushes in slowly.

  2. dogfight0.6s

    Wide aerial: the planes circle among the clouds above patchwork fields and chalk trenches, one Fokker trailing black smoke; a slow push in.

  3. cockpit0.8s

    Cowling camera with engine shake: Clawd looks back at the Fokker on its tail and raises an arm as the twin Vickers flash.

  4. enemy0.8s

    From the Fokker's upper wing, its guns firing, chasing Clawd's SPAD ahead; the camera rides fixed to the enemy plane.

  5. dive1.1s

    Over Clawd's shoulder as the SPAD dives: the upper wing and wires in front, shell craters and zig-zag trenches rushing up.

  6. clouds0.8s

    The SPAD, ghostly in the cloud bank, flies at the camera; the fog thickens until the frame whites out to cream.

Clawd is the American pilot: leather cap, goggles, white scarf, seated in the SPAD. The prologue hands over to the opening frame and the iris opens in the first beat; then five hard cuts, roughly every two beats. Around beat 5 Clawd looks back and the guns fire, and the cloud shot whites out into the cream that opens No. 2.

References: William A. Wellman, 1927 · Harry Perry (aerial photography) · Tinted silent-era release prints · RFC aerial photographs of the Somme, 1916–18

No.21928/29

The Broadway Melody

2DBlack-Gold Art Deco Revue Poster

  • Art Deco poster
  • Airbrush metallic gradients
  • A. M. Cassandre
  • Erté costume

An Art Deco revue poster in black, gold and silver: airbrushed gradients, a stepped proscenium, a chrome condenser mic. It is 1929 Broadway at the height of Deco, and the film where sound arrives, so rings of sound pour out of the mic.

Style prompt

Art Deco revue poster. Black lacquer ground; gold and silver from banded metallic gradients (3-5 hard bands); airbrush glows, sunbursts, ziggurat steps, streamlined curves; a stepped proscenium frame of gold lines and bulbs. Palette: ground #0A0908, gold #D6B560, highlight #F3E2A2, silver #BFC4C9; skin pale silver-cream; one saturated accent (e.g. #D97757) only on the hero the story centres on. Elongated Erté-style figures with real faces; fringe, sequins or feathers where the subject allows. Fine grain, glints, stars. Frontal symmetry, a central spotlight. Motion: chasing bulbs, a row of repeated elements (dancers, waves, columns) rippling toward the centre, rings radiating from the main sound or light source; slow pushes, cut on the music's beat (without music, every 2-3 s). References: A. M. Cassandre's posters, Erté's costumes. Avoid: flat yellow for metal, faceless figures, extra hues.

Shots & direction

  1. marquee0.8s

    Out of the white: the Astor Theatre marquee, 'THE BROADWAY MELODY / ALL TALKING · ALL SINGING · ALL DANCING', bulbs chasing; Clawd with boater and cane at left; a slow pull back.

  2. still1.0s

    The whole stage: Deco proscenium, chorus girls on the risers, Clawd tapping beside the giant condenser mic as it starts pouring out sound rings; a slow pull back.

  3. mic0.5s

    Close on the chrome spring-ring mic, rings radiating and notes flying; a push in.

  4. line0.5s

    Truck along the kick line: feathered headdresses, sequins, legs kicking in a wave.

  5. close0.5s

    Clawd in close-up, tapping, the boater tilting, the mic ring behind; a slight push in.

Clawd is the hoofer in a straw boater with a black cane, tapping all the way through. The film fades in from No. 1's white; on beat 4 the mic comes alive and the sound rings pour out (the talkies arrive). Four hard cuts run street → stage → mic → chorus line → Clawd.

References: Harry Beaumont, 1929 · 1929 MGM one-sheet · A. M. Cassandre · Erté

No.31929/30

All Quiet on the Western Front

2DB&WKollwitz Anti-war Woodcut

  • Woodcut
  • Käthe Kollwitz
  • German Expressionism
  • Two-block colour print

A woodcut after Käthe Kollwitz: big masses of black, white knife-cut contours, rough grain, with Clawd printed from its own orange block. Kollwitz's German anti-war prints of 1921–22 come from the same war and the same grief as the film.

Style prompt

Expressionist woodcut after Käthe Kollwitz, *Krieg* (1921-22). 60-70% solid black; white contour cuts 1/300-1/150 of frame width separate forms; faces are white shapes with a few black features; hands large. Figures fill the frame, cropped by its edges. Each cut tapers in and out with chipped edges, is wider the nearer it sits to the light, in slightly uneven bundles. Ink #17130F on paper #E8DFCA; texture is only wood-grain streaks and under-inked speckle (±5%). One colour block (e.g. #D97757) only on the hero the story centres on, flat on its body or clothes, cuts over it; black stays 60%+. Light enters through one opening (window, cloud gap); lamps get a short ring of cuts. The ground under figures stays uncut black. Quiet motion (breathing, a small gesture); slow pans and push-ins. Avoid: grey fog or midtones, even striped hatching, gore.

Shots & direction

  1. faces0.7s

    Pan along the trench past carved faces: one soldier with his face in his hands, one staring in fear, one asleep.

  2. poster0.4s

    Tight on a soldier's face under the steel helmet, staring straight out (a nod to the 1930 poster); a slow push in.

  3. clawd0.9s

    Medium: Clawd in a Stahlhelm on the firing step slowly reaches toward the loophole, where a white butterfly sits on a tin; a push in.

  4. butterfly0.7s

    Close on the white butterfly at the loophole, Clawd's orange arm entering from the left; a push in.

  5. field0.6s

    Wide over no-man's land to a cemetery of white crosses under a flare; a ghost column fades in, walking away; a slow pull back.

Clawd plays Paul in a German Stahlhelm. The film is held slow (1.2×) and quiet: the reach starts around beat 3 and takes three beats to stretch out, and the ghost soldiers appear only in the last shot. Four hard cuts go from the faces in the trench to Paul's arm, the butterfly and the field of crosses.

References: Lewis Milestone, 1930 · Käthe Kollwitz, Krieg (1921–22) · 1930 Universal poster

No.41930/31

Cimarron

2DSepia1889 Sepia Albumen Panorama

  • Gold-toned albumen print
  • Frontier panorama photograph
  • Hand-tinted figure

A gold-toned albumen panorama of 1889: soft lens, a little pan blur, cream highlights and umber shadows, silver grain; only Clawd is hand-tinted. Cimarron opens on the 1889 land rush, so it is shot as a photograph of that year, keeping the chapter monochrome.

Style prompt

A moving 1880s gold-toned albumen photograph. Paint soft-edged flat shapes by brightness: main subjects dark (15-30%), ground 50-60%, sky about 90%; visible hatching and grass tufts (lines ~1/300 of frame width). Then print: grayscale, slight blur (~1/800 of frame width), a faint smear of three horizontal offset copies growing with camera speed, multiply cream #F2DCB4, lift blacks to umber #2E1C0F, blotchy development (±5%), vignette to #785634, per-frame grain (±4%), dust, scratches. Hand-tint only the hero the story centres on (e.g. #D97757) as transparent dye, mask feathered 2-3 px; lamps stay sepia. Wide panorama: the middle band packed with repeated small elements (figures, animals, vehicles; for a lone subject fences, posts, trees), no big plane dominating. Camera: one 4-5 s ease-out pull back from the hero to the panorama. Avoid: hard ink outlines, colour beyond the hero, a flat grey print.

Shots & direction

  1. pull1.5s

    Starts tight on Clawd riding a black horse at the head of the run and pulls all the way back until the whole rush is in frame: schooners, a coach, a sea of riders in the dust.

  2. close1.0s

    Cut to Clawd close on horseback: it raises the Colt and fires into the air, the frame jolting with the recoil; a slight push in.

Clawd is Yancey Cravat in a white Stetson and a neckerchief, riding a black horse at the front. The run plays at 1.1×. The camera first pulls back to show more horses, then one cut to the close shot, where Clawd draws and fires on beat 5.

References: Wesley Ruggles, 1931 · Gold-toned albumen prints, 1880s · Currier & Ives (the flying gallop)

No.51931/32

Grand Hotel

3DB&WMGM Silver B&W 3D Lobby

  • three.js 3D
  • MGM silver-screen black and white
  • Art Deco interiors
  • Overhead symmetry

A three.js model of the circular lobby, graded in MGM's silver black and white with Clawd's orange as the only colour. The film's best-known image is the shot straight down the well, galleries closing in rings, so the set is built around it.

Style prompt

A real 3D set, by default an Art Deco interior (marble, chrome, chandelier, glossy checkerboard floor); outdoors keep the real setting with glossy surfaces (wet pavement, still water) and symmetrical framing. Ambient occlusion, soft bloom; 1930s studio silver: about 40% deep black, glossy whites near 90%, no washed-out greys. Light falls in shafts from one high source. All neutral grey except the hero the story centres on (e.g. #D97757), at least 1/6 of frame height; warm lamps render white, bloom only around them. Grain (±3%), halation. Signature shot: eye-height wide into a deep symmetrical set, floor and ceiling in frame, floor reflections; then 4-6 s tracks; one overhead on rings, figures as chess pieces. Period figures (heads, hats, limbs) walk routes. References: art director Cedric Gibbons, cinematographer William Daniels. Avoid: walking in place, frame-filling abstract patterns, colour casts.

Shots & direction

  1. overhead1.1s

    Straight down the six-gallery light well, the rings closing toward the key tower; the orange bellboy is a single dot crossing the checkerboard marble; a slow push in as the lens narrows.

  2. follow1.4s

    Side tracking shot at Clawd's height: it walks with its suitcase, the lobby sliding past, the revolving door turning ahead; the camera eases forward and in.

Clawd is the bellboy in a pillbox cap, carrying a suitcase covered in hotel labels. It really walks, 1.4 m/s at 2.4 strides a second, matched to the distance it covers. The film plays 1:1 with one hard cut at 1.08 s, from the overhead to the tracking shot.

References: Edmund Goulding, 1932 · Cedric Gibbons (art direction) · William Daniels (cinematography)

No.61932/33

Cavalcade

2DMucha Art Nouveau Panel

  • Alphonse Mucha
  • Colour lithograph
  • Art Nouveau ornament

A Mucha-style Art Nouveau panel: swelling brown outlines, pastel flats, a mosaic ground and a halo behind the central figure. Cavalcade opens on New Year's Eve 1899 with a family toasting the new century, so the halo is a clock striking twelve.

Style prompt

Alphonse Mucha colour lithograph. Brown contours #4B3326, about 1/250 of frame width, doubling on the shadow side (light from upper left), tapered ends; pastel flats, each with a hard-edged darker shadow tone. Palette: cream #F5ECD6 paper/sky, sage #A9B490 foliage/drapery, pale gold #CFA55A halo/ornament, rose #DBA49C skin/flowers; the hero the story centres on in one stronger accent (e.g. #D97757). Wide frame: figure and a big ring-filled halo in the middle third, arched panels either side, behind them a muted-teal (sage plus grey) square-tile mosaic; whiplash vines, local flowers; a bottom band of serif capitals in the user's language, 1/25 of frame height. No plain area over 1/8 of frame. Almond eyes, heavy lids; hair or cloth in three-tone strands. Litho grain, paper mottle, vignette. Slow sway; tilt down to the halo, pull back. Avoid: thin clean lines, saturated colour, empty backgrounds.

Shots & direction

  1. clock1.1s

    Tilt down from the CAVALCADE title and the galloping-horse frieze onto the clock halo, pushing in; the hands close on XII and the halo flares on the stroke.

  2. toast0.8s

    Medium on the toast: Clawd in a top hat and white bow tie springs up and raises its coupe while Jane and Robert raise theirs from either side; a slight pull back.

  3. still0.6s

    Pull back to the whole Mucha panel: glasses raised, rose petals falling, the band 'LONDON · XXXI DECEMBER MDCCCXCIX · A NEW CENTURY' at the foot.

Clawd plays one of the Marryots at the toast, in a silk top hat and white bow tie with a champagne coupe. The clock strikes twelve on beat 3 and the halo flares; the shot cuts straight to the toast, where Clawd hops and raises its glass. Played 1:1: two hard cuts, then a pull-back that settles on the whole poster.

References: Frank Lloyd, 1933 · Alphonse Mucha, The Seasons (1896) · Alphonse Mucha, Zodiac (1896) · Fox 1933 poster (the galloping horses)

No.71934

It Happened One Night

2DB&W1930s B&W Rubber-hose Cartoon

  • Fleischer rubber hose
  • Cel painting
  • Silly Symphonies watercolour backgrounds
  • Projected film wear

A 1934 black-and-white rubber-hose cartoon: thick-thin ink, pie-cut eyes, white gloves, cel shading over grey watercolour backgrounds, with film grain. It is the film's own year, and a road comedy gag suits cartoon timing.

Style prompt

Early-1930s black-and-white rubber-hose cartoon. Characters: noodle limbs, white gloves, pie-cut eyes, round heads; thick-thin ink #141414 (1/400-1/150 of frame width) swelling away from the light (default upper left); fills pure black and white plus at most one light grey for the hard shadow, gloss on blacks. Backgrounds stay mid-grey (30-80%), never pure black/white: graphite lines left in, grey watercolour washes with pooled edges and granulation, paper grain. Only the hero the story centres on has colour (e.g. #D97757): its whole body, or its one key prop if the hero is human. ±1 px gate weave, grain (±4%), flicker, scratches, dust. Actions anticipate, overshoot, settle: squash on landing, stretch on fast moves, eyes pop when surprised, a camera jolt on hits. References: Fleischer's Betty Boop shorts; Disney's Silly Symphonies backgrounds. Avoid: smooth vector lines, grey characters merging with the background.

Shots & direction

  1. thumb0.7s

    Close on Clawd's gloved thumb and smug face, the empty road behind, a car coming over the rise; a slow push in.

  2. two0.7s

    Two-shot: Clawd thumbing on the verge, Ellie on the fence rail hitching her skirt up to the calf; a slight pull back.

  3. road1.1s

    The payoff, wide: the car skids to a stop between them, the driver's eyes popping out; Clawd's eyes go wide, gloves open, hat jumping; the camera jolts on the stop.

Clawd plays Peter in a grey fedora, loosened striped tie and pencil moustache, with white gloves on its arm tips. Ellie lifts her skirt on beats 3–4; the car skids to a stop on beat 5, the one moment played at 1×, and Clawd's jaw-drop lands on it. Two hard cuts: thumb → two-shot → payoff.

References: Frank Capra, 1934 · Fleischer Studios, Betty Boop (1932–34) · Disney, Silly Symphonies backgrounds · 1934 Columbia one-sheet

No.81935

Mutiny on the Bounty

2DAdmiralty Sea Chart Copperplate

  • 18th-century Admiralty chart
  • Copperplate engraving
  • Thin hand colouring
  • Robert Dodd's aquatint

An 18th-century Admiralty sea chart engraved in copperplate, with rhumb lines, compass roses, soundings and thin hand colour; the Bounty is a ship vignette on the sheet. The film is a voyage, so the whole film stays on the chart.

Style prompt

A late-18th-century copperplate map, default an Admiralty sea chart (inland: a survey map). Fine burin hatching and cross-hatching (lines ~1/1200 of frame width) give all tone; a minimum line width makes small detail darken, not vanish. Ink #1C1610 on laid paper #EFE3C4, light foxing, folds. Thin hand colour: water #9EBCBC along edges, land #B8C08C; the hero the story centres on (e.g. #D97757) is the one strong colour. Rhumb lines from 32-point roses, a graduated border, hachured hills, cartouche, one ornament (e.g. a sea monster), serif lettering. Put the action in an unboxed engraved vignette fading into the chart, filling 60-75% of frame height at the end of the move. Camera: one 5-6 s glide from the whole sheet into the vignette, chart still around it; a dotted route inks itself in. Avoid: cropping the paper, heavy washes, stiff figures.

Shots & direction

  1. chart2.5s

    One continuous move: from the whole chart, with the Bounty sailing her dotted track, the camera glides in to the ship vignette, rhumb lines and roses still around it; on deck Clawd swings the cutlass up and points Bligh away as the launch falls astern and its track to Timor inks itself across the sheet.

Clawd is Fletcher Christian in a bicorne worn athwartships, cutlass in hand. The film never leaves the chart: one glide, no cuts. The cutlass goes up on beat 4 and levels at the launch a moment later with a glint, while the launch drifts astern through the last beats.

References: Frank Lloyd, 1935 · Robert Dodd, The Mutineers turning Lieut. Bligh… adrift (1790) · British Admiralty and Cook-voyage charts, 1770s–80s · Thomas Rowlandson

No.91936

The Great Ziegfeld

3DB&WSilver B&W 3D Wedding-cake Stage

  • three.js 3D
  • MGM silver black and white
  • 1936 revue spectacle
  • Ostrich feathers and chiffon

A three.js model of the revolving spiral 'wedding cake' stage in MGM's silver black and white: satin, chiffon and ostrich feathers against deep black, Clawd the only colour. The 175-step spiral of 'A Pretty Girl Is Like a Melody' is the film's famous set piece.

Style prompt

Real 3D stage spectacle in 1930s studio silver: the subject's world built as a revue set on a round tiered platform turning about 3° per second, draped and garlanded; its people become rows of costumed chorus figures with faces and arms. Costumes light satin grey, set mid-grey, pleated curtain backdrop darker, theatre deep black; whites below pure white. Soft front key; brighter rim lights edging satin and feathers near-white. Faint shafts, grain (±3%). All neutral grey except the hero the story centres on (e.g. #D97757); lamps render white. Signature shot: low medium along the tiers, figures 1/4-1/3 of frame height, the hero on the front tier. Then a 3 s curtain rise, a 10 s crane up to a level medium of the hero. Reference: Robert Z. Leonard, *The Great Ziegfeld* (1936). Avoid: flat lighting, faceless cones, fast spinning.

Shots & direction

  1. curtain0.9s

    Low front shot: the chiffon curtain flies up off the revolving cake, revealing tiers of fan-girls with Clawd tiny at the top; the camera tilts up.

  2. rise0.8s

    Crane up the spiral past the feather fans, a white grand piano and the violinists, stopping short of the top.

  3. top0.8s

    Near-level medium on Clawd at the summit in an ostrich-feather headdress: it turns to the camera and flings its arms up into a V; a slow push in.

Clawd is the star at the top of the cake, wearing a rhinestone band with a fountain of ostrich plumes. The scene runs at 1.2×, and the star is saved for the last cut: Clawd turns, hops and throws its arms into the V on beat 5. Two hard cuts link curtain, crane and summit.

References: Robert Z. Leonard, 1936 · Cedric Gibbons and John Harkrider (sets and costumes) · Oliver T. Marsh, Ray June, George Folsey (cinematography)

No.101937

The Life of Emile Zola

2DB&WNewsprint Letterpress and Line Engraving

  • Wood-type headline
  • Letterpress on newsprint
  • 1890s line engraving

Letterpress newsprint with 1890s line engraving: a wood-type headline, dense columns of lead type, ink spread and under-inking on yellowed paper, figures in hatching. The film's best-known image is L'Aurore's 'J'Accuse…!' front page of 13 January 1898.

Style prompt

1890s newspaper print. Set text as a sharp mask, then print: ~1 px blur and a noisy threshold for ink spread, ragged edges, under-inked patches, faint mirrored show-through. Page grammar: masthead, a wood-type headline across the width (~1/6 of frame height), 3-6 columns of small type in the user's language (~1/80 of frame height) flanking one large engraved picture. Wood-type letters are grained blocks. Everything pictured is a line engraving: hatching lines about 1/1000 of frame width, spaced 1/400, following the form, cross-hatching only in darks, heavier shadow-side contours, paler distance. Warm black #17130F on newsprint #E8E0CC; the hero the story centres on gets one accent plate (e.g. #D97757), offset 1-2 px. Motion: type blocks slam down, bounce and leave their print; one repeating mechanical motion, pages turning. Avoid: clean digital type, smooth grey shading, other colours.

Shots & direction

  1. headline0.9s

    Close on the L'Aurore front page dated 13 January 1898: the wood-type letters of 'J'Accuse…!' drop in one by one and the page jolts as the last one lands; a push in.

  2. clawd0.8s

    Medium on Clawd as Zola in front of the page: it looks up from its newspaper, springs up and thrusts the quill high, a newsboy shouting at right; a slight pull back.

  3. press0.8s

    The rotary press running, a printer holding up a wet sheet, papers flying out; the camera pulls back and trucks right.

Clawd is Zola in pince-nez, with a quill and that day's paper. The last letter lands on beat 2 and the page jolts; on beat 3 Clawd's head comes up and the quill shoots up. The scene runs at 1.05×, and two hard cuts go headline → Zola → press.

References: William Dieterle, 1937 · L'Aurore front page, 13 January 1898 · Le Petit Journal and L'Illustration line engravings · Marinoni rotary press

You Can't Take It with You

2DWPA Silkscreen Poster

  • 1930s WPA silkscreen
  • Split-fountain inks
  • Six flat opaque inks

A 1930s WPA silkscreen poster: six flat opaque inks, split-fountain gradients, slight misregistration on rag paper. The Federal Art Project posters of 1936–39 were American graphic design of the film's own years, and they keep it apart from No. 10's newsprint.

Style prompt

A 1936-39 WPA (Federal Art Project) silkscreen poster. Six opaque inks printed light to dark, each later screen cut away around lighter shapes (clouds, sun): cream #F0E2C0 for light, ochre #E0A842 and vermilion #C84A2A for sky and warm planes, teal #22686E for water and shade, the hero's own ink (e.g. #D97757, only on the hero the story centres on), brown keyline #322018 last. Shadows: a darker ink overprinted, never grey. Big areas get split-fountain blends: two inks in 2-3 coarse steps, the middle one their overprint. Screens 1-3 px off register; wandering stencil edges, squeegee streaks, warm rag-paper grain (±5%) on every flat. Figures fill 1/2-2/3 of frame height: big planes, crescent shadows, expressive faces. A bold capital title band, user's language. Slow pushes; screens pop in one by one. Avoid: halftone dots, smooth gradients, clean vector flats.

Shots & direction

  1. still0.8s

    The whole Vanderhof living room as a poster: Clawd as Grandpa in round glasses plays harmonica in the teal wing chair, Essie dances on pointe in the window, Kolenkhov shouts the count, De Pinna pops out of the cellar hatch; a slight pull back.

  2. harmonica0.6s

    Close on Clawd with glasses and harmonica, notes drifting up, De Pinna at the edge of frame; a push in.

  3. fireworks1.1s

    Back on the room as the cellar fireworks go off: rockets, starbursts and smoke columns fill it; the camera pulls back.

Clawd is Grandpa Vanderhof: round wire spectacles and a harmonica, crutches leaning on the chair. The whole household keeps doing its own thing at once while the cellar fireworks build through the last shot. Two hard cuts: room → harmonica → fireworks.

References: Frank Capra, 1938 · WPA Federal Art Project silkscreen posters, 1936–39 · 1938 Columbia one-sheet

No.121939

Gone with the Wind

2DTechnicolor Impasto Oil

  • Impasto oil
  • Palette knife
  • 1939 Technicolor palette
  • Epic poster painting

Impasto oil with palette-knife slabs, ridged paint and Technicolor heat: skies burning from gold to blood red, red earth, black silhouettes. Gone with the Wind is where colour arrives in the film, so Tara at sunset is the first fully saturated frame.

Style prompt

Impasto oil in a 1939 Technicolor palette. Palette-knife slabs: long horizontal sky bands (1/15-1/6 of frame width long, about 1/60 tall), shorter strokes following form on land and figures, calm thin areas between; ridges lit from upper left, paint thickest in highlights, canvas weave where thin. Default sunset sky: wine #3A0912, red #A8241A, orange #F27A22, gold #FFD878; ground red-brown #6E2214; warm near-black silhouettes. The hero the story centres on stays a readable warm mid-tone (e.g. #D97757), never a silhouette. Strong backlight, gold rims; in close shots a warm side light on half the face and hands. Epic-poster composition: big dark silhouettes (trees, buildings, rocks, figures) on a low horizon. Lamps as painted flames. Camera: slow push on the wide, low push-in on the hero, crane up to a vista. Avoid: tiled same-size strokes, muddy browns, smooth gradients.

Shots & direction

  1. tara1.7s

    Tara in silhouette: the great oak, the white house and cedars black against a sky burning from gold toward blood red; Clawd stands on a red-earth rise holding a radish; a slow push in.

  2. oath1.0s

    Low push-in against the burning sky: Clawd in a wide straw hat with a green ribbon raises the radish high, backlit with a gold rim.

  3. close0.4s

    Close on Clawd's face and hat, the ribbon tails whipping in the wind.

  4. fire1.5s

    Atlanta burning: the wagon charges past the flaming warehouse and timber frame, Clawd beside Rhett on the seat, Prissy and Melanie behind; the camera trucks with it as flames sweep the foreground.
